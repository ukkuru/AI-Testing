The situation

## A real company with a digital footprint that didn’t reflect it.

The client was not a startup finding its footing. They had a service portfolio, paying clients, and years of operational history. What they did not have was an SEO foundation that matched any of it.

Their website had pages. It had content. But there was no GA4 to measure what was working, no Google Search Console connected to catch indexation failures, and no structured data to tell search engines what the pages were actually about. Backlinks were sparse and unmanaged. Articles had not been shaped for the question-based formats that drive featured snippets and AI Overviews.

In the IT services space, this creates a specific problem. Buyers researching vendors use search engines and increasingly AI-generated answers to build their shortlist. If your company does not appear in those results, does not show structured data that signals authority, and has no local presence anchoring your credibility, you simply do not exist in the consideration phase. The sales team was working hard. The website was not working at all.

Root cause analysis

## Six gaps. One invisible website.

Before any work began, QualiPulse conducted a full audit across analytics, technical SEO, backlink profile, local presence, and content structure. The same six gaps appear repeatedly in mid-sized IT companies that have grown through referrals and word of mouth but never invested in organic search.

### No Analytics Foundation

GA4, Google Search Console, and Google Tag Manager were either not set up or configured incorrectly. No custom events. No conversion tracking. No data to make decisions from.

### Weak Backlink Profile

Domain authority was low and backlinks were few. No strategy existed for acquiring links from relevant, high-DA domains. Toxic links had accumulated without any disavow process in place.

### Technical SEO Debt

Core Web Vitals had never been tuned. Mobile usability had known issues. Internal linking was thin. Structured data was absent across all pages, leaving rich results completely untapped.

### No Local Search Presence

Google Business Profile had not been set up properly. Local citations were inconsistent. No strategy existed for building the regional trust signals that matter for IT services companies selling to local enterprise clients.

### Zero AEO and GEO Readiness

Existing articles were written for readers, not for AI search platforms. No FAQ schema. No structured answers. No alignment with the question patterns that drive Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity citations.

### No Indexation Oversight

New and updated pages were not being submitted to Google Search Console or Bing Webmaster Tools. Rankings were taking months longer to move than necessary because pages were waiting to be discovered, not actively pushed.

How we worked

## Structured execution. No surprises.

A five-stream technical engagement only works if the sequencing is right and the client has clear visibility at every step. Here is how the engagement was structured month by month.

1. M1

   ### Foundation Month: Analytics, Audit, and Baseline

   GA4, GSC, and GTM were set up in the first two weeks. Custom events went live for all key conversion actions. A full technical audit identified the 60 pages to be reworked over the engagement, prioritised by traffic potential and current page health. The first 12 pages, those with the most traffic potential, were reworked as soon as the audit was signed off. The backlink audit produced a disavow list submitted immediately. Google Business Profile work began, and the first regional citation was submitted. Baseline rankings were recorded across all target keywords.
2. M2

   ### Backlinks, On-Page, and First AEO Articles

   The first 25 backlinks were acquired from relevant DA 20-plus domains. Twelve more pages received full on-page treatment including Core Web Vitals fixes, internal links, and structured data, taking the total to 24. The first three articles were reworked for AEO, including FAQ schema, question-led headings aligned to AnswerThePublic data, and updated metadata. Pages were submitted to GSC and Bing Webmaster Tools immediately on update. The second regional citation went live.
3. M3

   ### Scale and Compound: Backlinks, Pages, Local Citations

   The second backlink batch added 25 more links, taking the total to 50, with anchor diversity monitored against the existing profile. Twelve more pages were reworked, taking the total to 36. The first regional landing page went live, targeting the client’s primary local market, the third local signal of the engagement. Weekly reporting was now generating actionable ranking data that informed prioritisation for months four and five. AEO articles four and five were completed and submitted.
4. M4

   ### Advanced Schema and GEO Refinement

   Advanced schema rollout across Article, FAQ, Breadcrumb, and Local Business types. Structured data was tested against Google’s Rich Results Test after each change. GEO alignment deepened as ranking data revealed which AI search platforms were citing which content formats. The fourth regional citation was submitted. Another 25 backlinks took the total to 75, the full backlink target. Another 12 pages took the total to 48. AEO articles six and seven followed. The cadence held.
5. M5

   ### Final Optimisation, Handover, and Ongoing Visibility

   The final 12-page batch was completed, bringing the total to 60 fully reworked pages. The last two AEO articles were delivered, completing all nine, and the fifth regional landing page went live. With the backlink target met, link work moved to monitoring. A complete handover document was produced covering all technical changes made, the backlink profile status, schema changes, and recommended next steps. The client now had a working analytics stack, a growing backlink profile, and content structured to appear in AI-generated search answers. Common questions

## What IT companies ask before starting a technical SEO engagement

What does a technical SEO engagement for an IT services company involve?

A technical SEO engagement covers Core Web Vitals work, structured data, internal linking, mobile usability, and indexation management. It also includes analytics setup with GA4 and Google Search Console, backlink acquisition from relevant domains, and AEO work to capture AI-generated search answers. The scope depends on the current state of the site and the priority pages. [See how QualiPulse structures technical SEO engagements](https://qualipulse.tech/content-engine/).

How long does it take for technical SEO to show results?

Analytics visibility is immediate once GA4 and GSC are correctly configured. Technical fixes produce ranking movement within 60 to 90 days as search engines re-crawl and re-index updated pages. Backlink authority builds over 3 to 6 months. AEO changes can produce AI search appearances faster, particularly for articles that already have some ranking history and are restructured around question-based search patterns.

What is GEO and AEO and why does it matter for IT services companies?

GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation) and AEO (Answer Engine Optimisation) are practices that structure content to appear in AI-powered search results from Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and similar platforms. For IT services companies, appearing in AI-generated answers positions the company as the authoritative answer before a buyer even reaches a traditional search result. This is where IT vendor shortlists are increasingly being formed. Read more about [building authority for AI search platforms](https://qualipulse.tech/build-thought-leadership-in-qa/).

Do I need content creation as part of a technical SEO engagement?

Not necessarily. This engagement was delivered entirely without new content production. The work focused on improving existing pages and articles for technical health, search intent alignment, and AI search readiness.
