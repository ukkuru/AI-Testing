## The rewritten article (ready to paste into WordPress)

When it comes to tea leaves and pregnancy, the short answer is that ordinary tea is fine in moderation, but the caffeine counts. The NHS and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) both use a limit of 200 mg of caffeine a day, which works out to about 2 to 4 cups of black tea depending on how strong and how big your cups are. Too much caffeine has been linked to a higher chance of miscarriage and low birth weight, and drinking tea with meals can reduce how much iron you absorb.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. Your midwife or doctor knows your health history and is the right person to ask about your own limits.*

## Tea leaves and pregnancy: what counts as tea

Black, green, white, oolong and matcha teas all come from the leaves of the same plant, Camellia sinensis. They all contain caffeine. Chai and most bubble teas are made with black tea, so they contain caffeine too.

Herbal teas such as peppermint, ginger, rooibos and chamomile are not made from tea leaves, so most of what you read about tea leaves and pregnancy is really about caffeine. They are naturally caffeine free, but they bring their own questions in pregnancy, which we cover below.

## How much caffeine is safe in pregnancy?

The [NHS advises](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/) pregnant women not to have more than 200 mg of caffeine a day, because too much caffeine has been linked to a higher chance of miscarriage and stillbirth. ACOG's [committee opinion on caffeine](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) says moderate intake below 200 mg a day does not appear to be a major factor in miscarriage or preterm birth, and that the link with slower baby growth is still not settled.

So 200 mg is a ceiling, not a target. Less is fine. And the 200 mg covers everything: tea, coffee, cola, energy drinks, chocolate and some cold remedies.

## Caffeine in tea: how much is in your cup?

Caffeine varies with the tea, the amount of leaf and how long you steep it. These are typical figures.

| Drink | Typical caffeine | Source |
|-|-|-|
| Black tea, 8 oz (240 ml) brewed | about 47 mg | USDA FoodData Central |
| Green tea, 8 oz (240 ml) brewed | about 29 mg (roughly 20 to 45 mg) | USDA FoodData Central |
| A cup of tea, black or green (UK mug) | about 75 mg | NHS |
| Chai or milk tea | similar to the black tea used | depends on recipe |
| Matcha | usually more than brewed green tea, because you drink the whole leaf | varies with the amount of powder |
| Decaf black or green tea | a small amount | check the label |
| Herbal teas (ginger, peppermint, rooibos, chamomile) | none | |
| Instant coffee, 1 cup | about 100 mg | NHS |
| Cola, 1 can | about 40 mg | NHS |
| Plain dark chocolate, 50 g | under 25 mg | NHS |

The NHS figure is higher than the USDA one because a typical British mug is bigger and brewed stronger. If you use two tea bags, a large mug or a long steep, assume the higher number.

Matcha deserves a separate note because you whisk and drink the powdered leaf. We cover it in detail in our guide to [matcha during pregnancy: benefits, risks and guidelines](https://teastory.blog/2025/11/11/matcha-during-pregnancy-benefits-risks-and-guidelines/).

## Can too much tea affect pregnancy?

Yes. Too much tea can affect pregnancy in three main ways, and caffeine is the biggest one.

### 1. Too much caffeine

Caffeine crosses the placenta, and a baby cannot clear it as quickly as an adult. Above about 200 mg a day, studies have linked caffeine to a higher chance of miscarriage, stillbirth and lower birth weight. Below that, the risk appears small.

### 2. Less iron absorbed

Your iron needs rise to 27 mg a day in pregnancy, according to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. Polyphenols in plant foods, including tea, can reduce how much iron your body absorbs from plant sources such as lentils, spinach and fortified cereals. A 2024 hospital study in Delhi found that drinking two or more cups of tea a day was one of the risk factors for anaemia in pregnant women, alongside not taking iron and folic acid tablets.

Easy fix: drink tea between meals rather than with them, and take your iron tablets with water or a vitamin C source instead of tea.

### 3. A possible effect on folate

Catechins in green tea have been shown in lab studies to interfere with folate. Human studies are mixed: a Japanese study found lower blood folate in pregnant women who drank a lot of green or oolong tea, while a study of Chinese pregnant women found no link. Taking your prescribed folic acid and keeping green tea moderate covers both possibilities.

## Tea leaves and pregnancy: how much tea is too much?

Here is what 200 mg of caffeine looks like in cups if tea is your only source.

| Tea | Caffeine per cup | Cups to reach 200 mg |
|-|-|-|
| Black tea, 8 oz, normal strength | about 47 mg | about 4 |
| Strong mug of tea (NHS estimate) | about 75 mg | about 2 to 3 |
| Green tea, 8 oz | about 29 mg | about 6 |
| Herbal tea | 0 mg | not limited by caffeine (see the 4 cup herbal guideline below) |

So when people search "does too much tea leaves affect pregnancy", the practical answer is this: more than about 4 standard cups of black tea, or more than 2 to 3 big strong mugs, takes most people past the limit. If you also drink coffee or cola, count those first.

### A sample day under 200 mg

* Morning: one mug of strong black tea (about 75 mg)
* Lunch: one can of cola (about 40 mg)
* Afternoon: one cup of green tea (about 29 mg)
* Evening: peppermint or rooibos tea (0 mg)

Total: about 144 mg, comfortably under the limit.

### Ways to keep enjoying tea

* Brew for 2 to 3 minutes instead of 5.
* Switch your second or third cup to decaf. Our [decaf tea guide](https://teastory.blog/2025/10/12/decaf-tea-guide-benefits-flavor-and-brewing-tips/) explains how it is made and how it tastes.
* Watch bottled and iced teas, which can hold more caffeine than you expect. See [how much caffeine is in iced tea](https://teastory.blog/2025/11/11/iced-tea-caffeine-content-what-you-need-to-know/).
* Use a smaller cup. An 8 oz cup is half of many travel mugs.

## Is tea good for pregnancy?

Tea is not a pregnancy supplement, but it can be a pleasant, hydrating part of your day. Tea counts toward your fluids, and a warm cup can be calming. Tea leaves also contain plant compounds called polyphenols, though there is no good evidence that they improve pregnancy outcomes, and very large amounts of green tea extract are not recommended. Drink tea because you enjoy it, keep the caffeine in check, and get your nutrients from food and prenatal vitamins.

## Herbal teas in pregnancy: what to limit and avoid

Being caffeine free does not make an herbal tea automatically safe. There is little research on most herbs in pregnancy. The NHS gives two clear rules:

* **Limit herbal teas such as peppermint and chamomile to no more than 4 cups a day.**
* **Avoid herbal teas that contain liquorice root.**

Reviews of herbal safety in pregnancy also advise avoiding teas with blue cohosh, black cohosh or pennyroyal, and any tea sold for weight loss, detox or as a laxative. Read the label on blends, because one box can hold several herbs.

Commonly used herbal teas:

* **Ginger:** some evidence it helps nausea (see below).
* **Peppermint:** often used for indigestion and wind; stay within the 4 cup limit.
* **Rooibos:** caffeine free and a good swap for black tea in the evening.
* **Chamomile:** within the 4 cup limit; avoid very large daily amounts.
* **Red raspberry leaf:** popular late in pregnancy. Research is limited, and many providers suggest waiting until later in pregnancy and asking first.

## Ginger tea for nausea

Ginger is the best studied herbal remedy for morning sickness. The NHS says there is some evidence that foods or drinks containing ginger may help reduce nausea and vomiting. A [2014 meta analysis of randomized trials](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3995184/) found ginger improved nausea compared with placebo, with no sign of increased risk to the pregnancy; its effect on the number of vomiting episodes was less clear.

A cup of fresh ginger tea is simple: simmer a few thin slices of fresh ginger in water for 5 to 10 minutes, then add lemon or a little honey. If you prefer a tea bag, a lemon and ginger blend works too; we reviewed [Twinings lemon and ginger tea](https://teastory.blog/2024/04/25/guide-to-twinings-lemon-and-ginger-tea-health-benefits/). Ask your pharmacist before taking ginger capsules or supplements, which are far stronger than tea. If you cannot keep fluids down for 24 hours, contact your midwife or doctor.

## FAQ

### Can too much tea leaves affect pregnancy?

Yes. More than about 200 mg of caffeine a day, roughly 4 standard cups of black tea or 2 to 3 strong mugs, has been linked to a higher chance of miscarriage and low birth weight. Lots of tea with meals can also lower iron absorption.

### Does tea affect pregnancy at all if I drink only one or two cups?

One or two cups of regular tea a day keeps you well under the 200 mg caffeine limit for most people. Just count any coffee, cola or energy drinks as well.

### Is tea good for pregnancy?

Tea is fine in moderation and helps with fluids, but it is not a health booster for pregnancy. Choose caffeine free herbal teas such as ginger or rooibos for extra cups.

### How many cups of green tea can I drink while pregnant?

A standard 8 oz cup of green tea has about 29 mg of caffeine, so up to about 6 cups stays under 200 mg if you have no other caffeine. Many people choose to stay well below that because of the possible effect on folate.

### Is matcha safe during pregnancy?

Matcha contains more caffeine than most brewed green tea because you drink the whole leaf, so it needs to fit inside the 200 mg limit. See our full guide to [matcha during pregnancy](https://teastory.blog/2025/11/11/matcha-during-pregnancy-benefits-risks-and-guidelines/).

### Which herbal teas should I avoid while pregnant?

Avoid teas with liquorice root, and herbs such as blue cohosh, black cohosh and pennyroyal. Keep other herbal teas to 4 cups a day or fewer, as the NHS advises.

### Does ginger tea help morning sickness?

There is some evidence that ginger helps nausea, and the NHS lists it as something you can try. Talk to a pharmacist before using ginger supplements.

## Bottom line

On tea leaves and pregnancy, moderation is the whole story. Keep total caffeine under 200 mg a day, which means about 4 standard cups of black tea or 2 to 3 strong mugs, and count coffee and cola too. Drink tea between meals so it does not get in the way of iron, keep taking your folic acid, limit herbal teas to 4 cups a day, avoid liquorice root, and try ginger tea for nausea. When in doubt, ask your midwife or doctor.
