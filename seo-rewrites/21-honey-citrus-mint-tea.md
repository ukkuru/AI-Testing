## The rewritten article (ready to paste into WordPress)

The real honey citrus mint tea benefits are comfort and mild symptom relief, not a cure. The warm drink soothes a scratchy throat, the honey has decent evidence for easing coughs, and it keeps you drinking fluids when you feel ill. The catch is sugar: a Grande from Starbucks has 30 g of sugar and 130 calories, so it works best as an occasional treat or a lighter custom order.

## What is honey citrus mint tea?

Honey Citrus Mint Tea is a Starbucks drink that started as a customer "secret menu" order called the Medicine Ball. Starbucks now lists it on the official menu and describes it as "a customer creation so popular it's now on the menu."

The original version mixed two Teavana tea bags, Jade Citrus Mint green tea and Peach Tranquility herbal tea, with hot water, steamed lemonade and honey. Baristas report that Peach Tranquility was discontinued and the recipe changed. Today the ingredient list on the [Starbucks Honey Citrus Mint Tea nutrition page](https://www.starbucks.com/menu/product/2122274/hot/nutrition) shows a single green tea infusion with apple, rose hips, lemon verbena, spearmint, chamomile flowers, licorice and lemongrass, plus lemonade and a honey blend syrup.

A Grande (16 fl oz) is made by default with two tea bags, two pumps of Honey Blend syrup, hot water and steamed lemonade.

The caffeine comes from the green tea. At 16 to 25 mg, a Grande has far less than a cup of brewed coffee, so it is a gentle choice in the afternoon.

## Honey citrus mint tea benefits: what it can actually help with

So what is honey citrus mint tea good for? Here is what holds up when you look at each part of the cup.

### Honey for coughs and a sore throat

This is the strongest part of the story. A 2021 systematic review in BMJ Evidence Based Medicine pooled 14 studies on [honey for upper respiratory tract infections](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32817011/). Compared with usual care, honey reduced how often people coughed and how bad the cough was, and improved overall symptom scores. The authors called it a cheap, widely available option for cold symptoms.

Keep the dose in mind. Those studies used honey itself, often a spoonful or more. Starbucks uses a honey blend syrup made from honey, water and natural flavors, and two pumps in a big cup is a modest amount. The effect in your cup is likely smaller than in the trials.

### Warmth and steam

A hot drink feels good when your throat is sore and your nose is blocked. A small study from the Common Cold Centre at Cardiff University, published in Rhinology in 2008, found that people with colds reported quick relief of runny nose, cough, sneezing and sore throat after a hot drink, compared with the same drink at room temperature. That relief is real but short lived, and it applies to any hot drink.

### Fluids when you are ill

Drinking enough matters when you have a cold. A warm, pleasant drink makes that easier, especially if plain water feels like a chore.

### Mint and chamomile

The blend uses spearmint, which is milder and has far less menthol than peppermint. The cooling taste can make your throat feel fresher, but there is no good evidence that this small amount clears congestion. Chamomile adds a calm, floral note. If you want a stronger mint cup, our guide to [Mint Majesty tea and its flavor](https://teastory.blog/2025/10/13/mint-majesty-tea-benefits-flavor-and-brewing-guide/) covers a peppermint and spearmint blend, and our post on [how to make spearmint tea](https://teastory.blog/2025/10/12/how-to-make-spearmint-tea-for-wellness-and-relaxation/) shows you the simple version at home.

### Green tea antioxidants

Green tea contains catechins, plant compounds studied for heart and metabolic health. Two tea bags shared with lemonade give you a small amount. It is a nice bonus, not a reason to drink the tea for health. For more on this pairing, read about [chamomile green tea and its benefits](https://teastory.blog/2025/10/12/chamomile-green-tea-benefits-for-relaxation-and-wellness/).

## The limits of honey citrus mint tea benefits

The name "Medicine Ball" oversells it. The drink will not:

* kill the virus behind a cold or flu, or make it end sooner
* "boost immunity" in any measurable way
* replace medicine your doctor has prescribed
* give you meaningful vitamin C, because the lemonade is mostly water, lemon juice and sugar

If you have a high fever, trouble breathing, or a sore throat that lasts more than a few days, see a doctor rather than relying on tea.

## Is honey citrus mint tea good for you?

Is honey citrus mint tea good for you? In moderation, yes, but the sugar is the big trade off.

The American Heart Association suggests no more than about 25 g of added sugar a day for most women and about 36 g for most men. A Grande has 30 g of sugar, and nearly all of it comes from the lemonade and the honey syrup, which count as added sugar. One cup can use up most or all of a day's limit.

That matters most if you are ill and drinking several cups a day, if you live with diabetes, or if you are watching your weight. For context on how sweet café drinks compare, see our honest [Starbucks chai review](https://teastory.blog/2025/10/13/starbucks-chai-review-love-it-or-hate-it/).

Two more cautions. Do not give honey in any form to babies under 12 months, because of the risk of infant botulism. And the licorice in the tea blend is a small amount, but people with high blood pressure who drink a lot of licorice products should check with their doctor.

## How to order a lower sugar version at Starbucks

You can keep most of the honey citrus mint tea benefits and cut the sugar with a few changes in the app or at the counter:

* **Ask for light lemonade or no lemonade.** Hot water takes its place, and most of the sugar goes with it.
* **Ask for fewer pumps of Honey Blend.** One pump instead of two in a Grande, or none at all.
* **Size down.** A Tall uses less syrup and lemonade than a Grande or Venti.
* **Add a lemon slice or two at home.** You get the citrus taste without the sugar.

Starbucks does not publish the nutrition for each custom order, so the app's nutrition panel is your best guide.

## Homemade honey citrus mint tea (copycat recipe)

Making it at home lets you use real honey, which is where most of the honey citrus mint tea benefits come from, and control the sugar. This makes one large mug, about 16 fl oz.

### Ingredients

* 1 green tea bag with mint, such as a citrus mint or spearmint green tea
* 1 chamomile or chamomile mint herbal tea bag
* 1 cup (240 ml) water just off the boil
* Juice of half a lemon plus 3/4 cup (180 ml) hot water, or 1/2 cup lemonade and 1/2 cup hot water for a closer match
* 1 to 2 teaspoons honey

### Method

1. Steep both tea bags in the hot water for 3 minutes. Green tea can turn bitter if you leave it much longer.
2. Remove the bags and add the lemon juice and extra hot water, or warm lemonade.
3. Let it cool for a minute, then stir in the honey to taste.
4. Add a slice of lemon or a sprig of fresh mint if you have one.

With fresh lemon and 2 teaspoons of honey, you will have roughly 12 g of sugar, about 40% of the Grande's 30 g. For a caffeine free night version, swap the green tea for a peppermint or spearmint herbal bag.

## FAQ

### What are the main honey citrus mint tea benefits?

The main honey citrus mint tea benefits are soothing warmth, extra fluids and some cough relief from honey. It eases symptoms but does not cure a cold or flu.

### Is honey citrus mint tea good for you?

It is fine as an occasional drink, but a Grande has 30 g of sugar, close to or above a day's recommended added sugar. A lighter order or a homemade version is a better daily choice.

### What does honey citrus mint tea help with?

It helps most with a sore or scratchy throat and a cough, thanks to the honey and the warmth. It can also make it easier to stay hydrated when you feel unwell.

### Does honey citrus mint tea have caffeine?

Yes, a little. Starbucks lists 16 to 25 mg of caffeine in a Grande, from the green tea.

### How many calories are in a Grande honey citrus mint tea?

A Grande has 130 calories, 33 g of carbohydrates and 30 g of sugar, according to Starbucks.

### Why is it called the Medicine Ball?

Customers gave it that nickname because they ordered it when they had a cold. Starbucks added it to the menu as Honey Citrus Mint Tea.

## Bottom line

Honey citrus mint tea benefits are about comfort: warmth for a sore throat, honey for a cough and an easy way to drink more fluids. The evidence for honey is reasonable, but the drink is not medicine and it carries 30 g of sugar in a Grande. Order it with light lemonade and fewer pumps of honey, or make it at home with real honey and fresh lemon, and save the full sweet version for the days you really want it.
