# Does Herbal Tea Break a Fast? A Tea by Tea Answer

Plain, unsweetened herbal tea does not break a fast for most people. Barley, peppermint, chamomile, fennel, rooibos, ginger and hibiscus teas have close to zero calories and no sugar, so they will not meaningfully raise insulin. The exceptions: added honey, milk or fruit, a blood test that says "water only", and religious fasts that exclude all drinks.

The longer answer depends on what is in your cup and why you are fasting.

## First, what are you fasting for?

"Break a fast" means different things depending on your goal. Find yours here before you look at the tea table.

* **Weight loss or blood sugar (16:8, OMAD, 5:2).** The usual rule is to stay close to zero calories and avoid sugar so insulin stays low. Plain tea, herbal or not, fits.
* **Autophagy or a longer "clean" fast.** Nobody has measured exactly how many calories switch autophagy off in humans. If this is your goal, the cautious move is water, plain black coffee or plain unsweetened tea, and nothing with calories at all.
* **A fasting blood test.** Follow your lab's instructions, not the internet. Most fasting glucose and cholesterol tests ask for water only, typically for 8 to 12 hours. Tea, even herbal, can be a problem for some tests, so skip it unless your doctor says it is fine.
* **A religious fast.** The rules come from your tradition, not from biology. During Ramadan, for example, nothing passes the lips from dawn to sunset, including water and tea. Other traditions allow drinks. If you are unsure, ask your faith leader.

## Does barley tea break a fast?

No. Barley tea is made by steeping roasted barley grains in hot water, and the grains stay behind when you pour. What ends up in the cup is mostly water with a toasty, nutty flavour and close to zero calories. It is caffeine free too, which is why it is a daily drink in Japan and Korea.

The catch is bottled barley tea. Most is unsweetened, but some brands add sugar. If the label lists sugar, save it for your eating window.

## Does licorice root tea break a fast?

Not in the calorie sense. Licorice root tastes very sweet because of a compound called glycyrrhizin, which is many times sweeter than sugar, but a cup of plain licorice tea carries almost no calories. There is no good evidence that a normal cup knocks you out of a fasted state.

The real issue is your heart, not your fast. Eating or drinking a lot of real licorice root over weeks can raise blood pressure and lower potassium. That risk matters more if you already have high blood pressure, heart or kidney problems, or you are pregnant. One cup now and then is not the concern; several strong cups every fasting day is. If any of those apply to you, choose peppermint or rooibos instead.

## Does peppermint tea break a fast?

No. Plain peppermint tea has no sugar and virtually no calories. The menthol hits your taste buds hard, which many people find helps when hunger peaks in the last hours of a fast. It is caffeine free, so it is also a good evening option.

One small note: peppermint relaxes the muscle at the top of your stomach, so if you get reflux on an empty stomach, it can make heartburn worse.

## Does fennel tea break a fast?

No. Fennel seed tea is calorie free when brewed plain, with a mild aniseed flavour that reads as slightly sweet without any sugar. It is a traditional remedy for bloating, though the evidence is thin, so treat it as a comfort drink rather than a treatment.

## Does chamomile tea break a fast?

No. Chamomile is caffeine free and calorie free, so you can drink it at any point in your fasting window, including late evening when snack cravings hit.

## Does green tea break a fast?

Plain green tea does not break a fast for weight loss or blood sugar goals. A cup has about 2 calories and no sugar. (It is not technically herbal, since it comes from the tea plant, but the answer is the same.)

Two things to watch:

* **Caffeine.** Green tea has caffeine, usually around 30 to 50 mg a cup. On an empty stomach, some people get jittery or a little nauseous. If that is you, drink it closer to your eating window or brew it weaker. More on that in [our post on caffeine in teas during fasting](https://teastory.blog/2025/10/26/is-there-caffeine-in-herbal-tea-during-fasting/).
* **Stomach upset.** The tannins in strongly brewed green tea can irritate an empty stomach. Steep it for 2 to 3 minutes with water just off the boil to keep it gentle.

## Milk, honey and lemon: where the fast actually breaks

* **Milk.** Even a splash brings calories, protein and lactose. For a flexible weight loss fast, some people accept it. For autophagy, a blood test or a strict clean fast, it breaks the fast.
* **Honey and sugar.** These break a fast. A teaspoon of honey is around 20 calories of sugar, enough to nudge insulin.
* **Lemon.** A squeeze adds a calorie or two. Most people doing intermittent fasting treat it as fine. Strict fasters and anyone before a blood test should leave it out.
* **Sweeteners (stevia, sucralose).** They add no calories, but the evidence on whether they affect appetite or insulin is mixed. If you are strict, avoid them. If a little stevia keeps you on track with a 16:8 plan, that is a reasonable trade.

## Is herbal tea the same as drinking water?

Close enough for most fasts. Herbal tea is mostly water and counts towards your hydration, but its plant compounds are why blood tests and strict fasts still say water only. We cover the hydration side in more detail in [is drinking herbal tea the same as drinking water](https://teastory.blog/2025/10/26/is-drinking-herbal-tea-the-same-as-drinking-water/).

## Tips for drinking tea while fasting

* Read the ingredients of any blend. Fruit pieces, sugar and honey granules break a fast.
* Keep caffeinated tea earlier in the day.
* Drink plain water too. Tea adds to water during a fast; it does not replace it.
* If you take medicines, are pregnant, or have diabetes, check with your doctor before longer fasts, and before using licorice tea regularly.

## FAQ

### Do herbal teas break a fast?
Plain herbal teas such as peppermint, chamomile, fennel, rooibos, ginger and barley do not break an intermittent fast. They have close to zero calories and no sugar. They can break a strict water only fast, a religious fast that excludes all drinks, or a fasting blood test.

### Does barley tea break a fast?
No. Plain barley tea is roasted barley steeped in water, with close to zero calories and no caffeine. Avoid bottled barley tea with added sugar during your fasting window.

### Does licorice root tea break a fast?
Not by calories. It tastes sweet because of glycyrrhizin, not sugar. But heavy, regular use of licorice root can raise blood pressure and lower potassium, so limit it if you have high blood pressure or heart or kidney problems, or are pregnant.

### Does peppermint tea break a fast?
No. Plain peppermint tea has no sugar and virtually no calories, and it is caffeine free. It can worsen heartburn in people prone to reflux.

### Can you drink green tea while fasting?
Yes. Plain green tea has about 2 calories a cup and will not break an intermittent fast. It contains caffeine, so some people feel jittery or queasy if they drink it strong on an empty stomach.

### Does fennel tea break a fast?
No. Plain fennel tea is calorie free. Many people drink it during a fast because it can help with bloating.

### Can I drink chamomile tea while fasting?
Yes. Chamomile is calorie free and caffeine free, so you can drink it at any time in your fasting window, including the evening.

## Bottom line

For everyday intermittent fasting, plain herbal tea is fine, and so is plain green or black tea. Barley, peppermint, fennel, chamomile, rooibos, ginger and hibiscus are all safe bets. Licorice root will not break your fast, but go easy on it if your blood pressure is a concern. Milk, honey and sugar are where a fast really breaks. And if you are fasting for a blood test or a religious fast, the rules of your lab or your faith come first, which often means water only or nothing at all.
