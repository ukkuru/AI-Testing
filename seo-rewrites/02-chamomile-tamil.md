## The rewritten article (ready to paste into WordPress)

Chamomile in Tamil is **சாமந்தி (saamanthi)**. The Tamil Lexicon lists "wild chamomile, Matricaria chamomilla" as one meaning of the word. The catch: in everyday Tamil, சாமந்திப் பூ usually means the garden chrysanthemum used in garlands. So when you shop for chamomile tea, look for the English name "chamomile" on the pack, or the Tamil spelling கெமோமில் (kemomil).

Below you will find every Tamil name you are likely to see, how to tell real chamomile from the flower market saamanthi, how to make the tea, and what the research actually says about its benefits.

Camomile and chamomile are the same word. Camomile is simply the older British spelling, so "camomile in Tamil" gives you the same answer.

## Chamomile meaning in Tamil: why saamanthi gets confusing

Chamomile and chrysanthemum belong to the same plant family, the daisy family (Asteraceae). Both have a ring of petals around a central disc, so Tamil used one word for both.

In practice, the flower you buy at a Tamil Nadu flower market as சாமந்தி is almost always chrysanthemum. It is larger, comes in bright yellow, white or purple, and is used for garlands and pooja. It is not what goes into chamomile tea.

Here is how to tell them apart:

| Feature | Chamomile | Garden saamanthi (chrysanthemum) |
| - | - | - |
| Botanical name | Matricaria chamomilla (German), Chamaemelum nobile (Roman) | Chrysanthemum species |
| Flower size | Small, about 1 to 2 cm across | Larger, often 3 to 8 cm |
| Petals | White petals, raised yellow centre that is hollow inside | Many layered petals, often all yellow or white |
| Smell | Sweet, like apples | Green, slightly bitter |
| Main use | Herbal tea, skin care | Garlands, pooja, decoration |

The name chamomile comes from Greek words meaning "earth apple", because of that apple smell. If your dried flowers do not smell faintly of apple, they are probably not chamomile.

## Chamomile plant in Tamil Nadu

Chamomile is native to Europe and western Asia. It likes cool, mild weather, which is why most chamomile tea sold in India is imported, commonly from Egypt or Europe. It is grown commercially in a few parts of India, but it is not a traditional Tamil Nadu crop and you will not find it growing wild on the plains.

That is also why traditional Tamil Siddha medicine does not feature chamomile in the way it features tulsi, vetiver or coriander. Chamomile is a newer arrival in Tamil kitchens. It fits in easily, though, because Tamil homes already have a long habit of drinking herbal kashayam and evening infusions.

## Chamomile tea in Tamil: how to make it

You need just two things: dried chamomile flowers (or a tea bag) and hot water.

1. Heat 250 ml (one cup) of water until it just boils, then let it sit for a few seconds.
2. Add 1 heaped teaspoon to 1 tablespoon of dried flowers, or one tea bag.
3. Cover the cup and steep for 5 minutes. Covering keeps the aroma in.
4. Strain. Add a little honey, palm sugar (karupatti) or a slice of lemon if you like.
5. Drink it 30 to 60 minutes before bed if you are using it to wind down.

Steeping much longer than 7 or 8 minutes can make it taste flat and slightly bitter. If you want it stronger, add more flowers rather than steeping longer.

For a summer version, steep double strength, cool it, and pour over ice with a few mint leaves. If you like spice, a crushed cardamom pod or a thin slice of ginger works well too. ### Buying tips

* Whole flower heads beat dusty tea bag fines for taste.
* The pack should say Matricaria chamomilla, German chamomile or simply chamomile. Avoid anything labelled only "saamanthi" unless it clearly says chamomile.
* Look for a recent packing date. Chamomile loses its aroma within months, faster in humid weather.

## Chamomile tea benefits: what the research says

Chamomile is one of the most studied herbal teas, but most studies are small. Here is the honest picture.

### Sleep

Chamomile contains apigenin, a plant compound that binds to the same brain receptors that calming medicines act on, though far more weakly. Small studies in older adults and new mothers found modest improvements in sleep quality. It will not knock you out, and it is not a replacement for medical treatment of insomnia. As a caffeine free evening drink in place of milk tea or coffee, it helps.

### Stress and mild anxiety

A few clinical trials using chamomile extract (capsules, not tea) found modest reductions in anxiety symptoms compared with placebo. Tea is much weaker than those extracts, so expect a calming ritual rather than a treatment.

### Digestion

Chamomile has a long history of use for indigestion, gas and stomach cramps across European and Asian folk medicine. Good clinical trials are limited, but a warm cup after a heavy meal is a low risk habit many people find soothing.

### Skin

Applied to the skin, chamomile shows mild anti inflammatory effects in lab and small human studies. A cooled tea rinse is a gentle option for minor irritation. Stop if you see redness or itching.

If you want a broader comparison, our post on the [health benefits of different types of tea](https://teastory.blog/2024/05/13/the-health-benefits-of-different-types-of-tea/) puts chamomile alongside green, black and other herbal teas.

## Chamomile tea side effects

Chamomile is safe for most adults in normal amounts (one to three cups a day), but check these first:

* **Daisy family allergy:** if you react to chrysanthemum, marigold or ragweed pollen, you may react to chamomile too. This matters in Tamil Nadu, where people handle saamanthi and marigold garlands often.
* **Blood thinners:** chamomile may interact with warfarin and similar drugs. Ask your doctor.
* **Sedatives and sleep medicines:** it can add to their drowsy effect.
* **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** there is not enough safety data for regular or large amounts. Check with your doctor.
* **Babies and young children:** ask your paediatrician first, and never give honey to a baby under one year.

## FAQ

### What is chamomile called in Tamil?
Chamomile is சாமந்தி (saamanthi) in Tamil, according to the Tamil Lexicon. Because saamanthi also means chrysanthemum, many people simply use the English word, written கெமோமில் (kemomil).

### What is chamomile flower in Tamil?
Chamomile flower is சாமந்திப் பூ (saamanthi poo). In shops and flower markets that phrase usually means chrysanthemum, so ask for "chamomile" or "kemomil" to get the tea flower.

### What is chamomile tea in Tamil?
Chamomile tea is கெமோமில் டீ (kemomil tea) or சாமந்தி தேநீர் (saamanthi theneer). Most packs in India use the English name.

### Is chamomile and saamanthi the same?
Partly. The dictionary gives saamanthi as a name for chamomile, but the saamanthi sold for garlands is chrysanthemum, a related but different plant. Do not brew garland flowers as chamomile tea.

### What is chamomile oil in Tamil?
Chamomile oil is கெமோமில் எண்ணெய் (kemomil ennai). It is an essential oil for diffusers or diluted skin use, never for drinking.

### Can I drink chamomile tea every day?
Yes, one to three cups a day is fine for most healthy adults. Skip it if you are allergic to daisy family plants, and check with your doctor if you take blood thinners or sedatives, or are pregnant.

### Does chamomile tea break a fast?
Plain chamomile tea has almost no calories, so most people treat it as fine during intermittent fasting. Adding honey or sugar does break a fast. There is no good research on chamomile and autophagy specifically.

## Bottom line

Chamomile in Tamil is சாமந்தி (saamanthi), but because that word also covers the chrysanthemum in your pooja garland, ask for "chamomile" or கெமோமில் when you buy it. Brew a teaspoon or two of dried flowers for five minutes, drink it in the evening, and treat it as a gentle, caffeine free way to unwind, not a cure. If you have a daisy family allergy or take regular medicines, check with your doctor first.
